================================================================================
              HCMR — VISITOR REQUEST DECISION SUPPORT MATRIX
================================================================================

Date:        15 April 2026
Prepared By: Capt James Bird, Division Commander, HCMR
Classification: OFFICIAL

PURPOSE
-------
To provide a consistent, transparent framework for assessing whether a
proposed visit to the Household Cavalry Mounted Regiment delivers genuine
value or creates an unjustified burden on personnel and resources.


================================================================================
SECTION 1: SCORING CRITERIA
================================================================================

Score each criterion 0-3:

  0 = None / Not applicable
  1 = Low / Marginal
  2 = Moderate / Useful
  3 = High / Significant


CRITERION 1: RELATIONSHIP VALUE
--------------------------------
Does the visitor belong to an organisation with an existing or potential
relationship that benefits HCMR?

  3  Formal partnership or affiliation (e.g. Livery Company with historic
     ties, allied regiment, MOD stakeholder, DSVS, Defence Animal Trust)
  2  Organisation with shared interests and potential for future
     partnership (e.g. equine charity, veterinary institution)
  1  Loose connection or goodwill link (e.g. local council, general
     military association)
  0  No organisational affiliation to HCMR


CRITERION 2: OPERATIONAL BENEFIT
---------------------------------
Does the visit deliver a tangible outcome for the regiment?

  3  Direct operational gain (e.g. funding, equipment, veterinary
     expertise, training support, policy influence)
  2  Useful exchange of knowledge or capability (e.g. equine welfare
     research, farriery techniques, saddlery expertise)
  1  Minor benefit such as general awareness or morale uplift
  0  No identifiable operational benefit


CRITERION 3: REPUTATIONAL VALUE
---------------------------------
Does the visit enhance HCMR's public profile, recruitment, or standing
within Defence and the Crown?

  3  High-profile positive exposure (e.g. Royal visit, senior Defence
     figure, reputable media with editorial control)
  2  Moderate positive exposure (e.g. established journalist, respected
     public figure with genuine interest)
  1  Limited or niche exposure (e.g. social media personality, local
     press)
  0  No reputational benefit, or risk of negative/trivialised coverage


CRITERION 4: RECIPROCITY
--------------------------
Is the relationship two-way? Does HCMR gain as much as it gives?

  3  Clear mutual benefit (e.g. Livery Company provides funding/support,
     HCMR provides ceremonial connection and access)
  2  Visitor offers something in return (e.g. expertise, future
     sponsorship, charitable support)
  1  Visitor is primarily a recipient but goodwill may pay off
  0  Entirely one-sided — visitor gains access/content, HCMR gains nothing


CRITERION 5: DISRUPTION COST
------------------------------
How much does the visit cost in time, personnel, and operational impact?
(NOTE: This criterion is scored in REVERSE — lower disruption = higher score)

  3  Minimal disruption (e.g. short visit during routine activity,
     no dedicated escort beyond normal duties)
  2  Moderate disruption (e.g. requires a dedicated host for 1-2 hours,
     minor schedule adjustment)
  1  Significant disruption (e.g. half-day programme, multiple personnel
     diverted, horses prepared specifically)
  0  Major disruption (e.g. full-day event, rehearsal required,
     operational activity displaced)


================================================================================
SECTION 2: DECISION THRESHOLDS
================================================================================

  Total Score (out of 15)    Recommendation
  -------------------------  ----------------------------------------
  12 - 15                    APPROVE — clear value to HCMR
   9 - 11                    APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS — value exists but
                             scope/timing should be managed to minimise
                             disruption
   6 - 8                     REFER UPWARDS — marginal value, requires
                             CO/Adjt decision with justification
   0 - 5                     DECLINE — insufficient benefit to justify
                             the cost; suggest alternative (e.g. public
                             Changing of the Guard, museum visit)

================================================================================
SECTION 4: PROCESS
================================================================================

  1. Visitor request received (by Adjutant, Div Comd, or external liaison)
  2. Requesting officer completes scoring matrix (5 criteria, 0-3 each)
  3. Score totalled and recommendation identified:
     - APPROVE / APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS / REFER UPWARDS / DECLINE
  4. For REFER UPWARDS: brief submitted to CO/Adjt with completed matrix
  5. Decision recorded and communicated to requesting party
  6. If approved: assign host officer, confirm date, brief visitors on
     dress/security/photography policy
  7. Post-visit: note any follow-up actions (thank you letter, future
     engagement, partnership development)


================================================================================
SECTION 5: ADDITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS
================================================================================

The matrix provides a baseline. The following factors may override or
adjust the score at the CO's discretion:

  - Royal or Crown direction (automatic approve regardless of score)
  - Chain of Command direction (e.g. Brigade or Div HQ request)
  - Security concerns (may decline an otherwise high-scoring visit)
  - Operational tempo (during Major General's Inspection, State
    Ceremonial periods, or deployment — raise the threshold to 11+)
  - Cumulative burden (if multiple visits are stacking up in the same
    week, apply stricter thresholds to protect training time)
