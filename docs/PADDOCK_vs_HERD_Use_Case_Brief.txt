PURPOSE
-------
To demonstrate, through practical use cases, why PADDOCK is a more
effective horse management system for HCMR than HERD.


================================================================================
WHAT IS PADDOCK?
================================================================================

PADDOCK is a purpose-built digital horse management system designed
specifically for the Household Cavalry Mounted Regiment. It runs as a
web application accessible from any device with a browser — phone, tablet,
or desktop — with no software installation required.

It was developed in-house by an officer within the regiment, meaning it
reflects how we actually work rather than how a civilian software vendor
thinks we work.


================================================================================
THE CORE PROBLEM WITH HERD
================================================================================

HERD is a generic equine management tool. It was not designed for a
military mounted unit. The consequence is that it does not understand:

  - Our rank structure and chain of command
  - Our duty stations (King's Life Guard, Training Wing, Hyde Park
    Barracks, Winter Training)
  - Our horse roles (Charger, Cav Black, Grey, Standard, Comp, RMT)
  - Our squadrons (The Life Guards, The Blues and Royals)
  - Our permission structure (what a Trooper can do vs a Vet vs an Officer)
  - Our operational rhythm (ceremonial commitments, guard rotations,
    training cycles)

PADDOCK was built from the ground up around all of these.

USE CASE 1: INJURY REPORTING
================================================================================

SCENARIO: A Trooper notices a horse is lame during morning stables.

WITH HERD:
  - Trooper tells the Corporal of Horse verbally
  - CoH writes it in the yard book or sends a WhatsApp message
  - Information may or may not reach the vet that day
  - If it does, there is no photo, no structured record, no timestamp
  - No audit trail of who knew what and when

WITH PADDOCK:
  - Trooper opens PADDOCK on their phone
  - Taps "Report Injury" on the horse's profile
  - Selects severity (Minor/Moderate/Severe), body location, description
  - Attaches up to 5 photos directly from the phone camera
  - Submits — the report is timestamped and attributed to the Trooper
  - ALL vets and admins are automatically notified immediately
  - If severity is SEVERE: the horse's task readiness is automatically
    downgraded to NON-TASKWORTHY
  - If severity is MODERATE: the horse is automatically moved to
    LIMITED ROLE (if currently at Full Exercise)
  - Full audit trail: who reported it, when, what was done about it

WHY IT MATTERS:
  The Commanding Officer can see exactly how long it took from injury
  to report to veterinary attention. If a horse is injured at 0600 and
  the vet doesn't see the report until 1400, that is visible. With HERD
  or paper, that gap is invisible.

USE CASE 2: "HOW MANY HORSES CAN WE MOUNT FOR KING'S LIFE GUARD?"
================================================================================

SCENARIO: The Adjutant needs to know at short notice how many horses
are available for a ceremonial commitment next week.

WITH HERD:
  - Ring the yard
  - Ask the CoH to count heads
  - Cross-reference against the sick board
  - Check if any are on medication withdrawal
  - Hope nobody forgot to update the whiteboard
  - Manually compile the answer — takes 30+ minutes and may be wrong

WITH PADDOCK:
  - Open the Dashboard
  - Task Readiness chart shows immediately:
    * X horses at FULL EXERCISE (available)
    * Y horses at LIMITED ROLE (may be available depending on task)
    * Z horses NON-TASKWORTHY (unavailable)
  - Open Injuries page — filter by OPEN status to see current casualties
  - Check Active Withdrawals panel — see which horses are on medication
    and when withdrawal ends
  - Open Location Planner — select the date, filter by squadron and role,
    see exactly which horses will be at which location on that date,
    including any in transit

  Answer in under 2 minutes, with confidence, from anywhere.

USE CASE 3: HORSE MOVES — WINDSOR, PIRBRIGHT, MELTON MOWBRAY
================================================================================

SCENARIO: 12 horses need to move to Melton Mowbray for training.
The Adjutant needs to plan the transport.

WITH HERD:
  - Spreadsheet or email chain
  - No visibility of which horses are already moving elsewhere
  - Driver details, vehicle registration, box groom details all on
    separate paperwork
  - No one place to see the full picture

WITH PADDOCK:
  - Create a group move: select the 12 horses, set departure/arrival
    dates, assign driver (name + service number), box groom, vehicle VRN
  - The move appears on every horse's individual profile under "Moves"
  - The Location Planner automatically updates the projected location
    for each horse on the move date
  - Status tracking: PLANNED → IN TRANSIT → COMPLETED
  - When completed, each horse's current location updates automatically
  - If a move is cancelled, status is updated and the location projection
    reverts

  One person plans the move. Everyone can see it. No duplication.

USE CASE 4: VETERINARY HEALTH SCHEDULE
================================================================================

SCENARIO: The vet needs to know which horses are due for farriery,
dental checks, or vaccinations.

WITH HERD:
  - Separate spreadsheet or calendar
  - Relies on someone manually checking and chasing
  - Easy for events to slip without anyone noticing

WITH PADDOCK:
  - Health Schedule page shows all upcoming and overdue events across
    the entire fleet
  - Filter by type (Dental, Vet Checkup, Vaccination, Farriery)
  - Filter by status (Scheduled, Overdue, Completed)
  - OVERDUE events are flagged in red on the Dashboard — visible to
    anyone who logs in
  - When a health event is completed, PADDOCK automatically schedules
    the next occurrence based on the event type's frequency
  - Each horse's profile shows their full health history in one place

  Nothing falls through the cracks. Overdue events are visible to
  everyone, not hidden in a spreadsheet only one person checks.

USE CASE 5: ROLE-BASED ACCESS — WHO CAN DO WHAT
================================================================================

SCENARIO: A Trooper wants to edit a horse's profile. A vet wants to
prescribe medication. An officer wants to assign a horse to duty.

WITH HERD:
  - Either everyone has the same access (risky) or access is so
    restricted that people can't do their jobs without workarounds

WITH PADDOCK:
  Four roles with precisely defined permissions:

  TROOPER:
    - View all horse records
    - Report injuries (with photos)
    - Add health notes
    - Upload documents
    - Cannot edit horse profiles, prescribe medication, assign duties,
      or manage users

  VET:
    - Everything a Trooper can do, plus:
    - Create and update health events
    - Prescribe and record medication (with batch numbers and withdrawal
      tracking)
    - Manage feeding plans
    - Resolve injury reports
    - Conduct and record inspections
    - Cannot assign duties or manage users

  OFFICER:
    - Create and edit horse profiles
    - Assign horses to duty stations
    - Assign riders (with suitability scoring)
    - Manage tack allocations
    - Approve and manage horse moves
    - Manage locations
    - Cannot prescribe medication or manage users

  ADMIN:
    - Full access to everything
    - User management (create accounts, assign roles, deactivate users)
    - System configuration
    - Audit log visibility

  This means a Trooper cannot accidentally change a horse's weight, a vet
  cannot reassign a horse to a different squadron, and an officer cannot
  alter a medication record. Everyone can do their job, no one can do
  someone else's.

USE CASE 6: MEDICATION WITHDRAWAL TRACKING
================================================================================

SCENARIO: A horse has been given bute. The vet needs to ensure it is not
tasked for ceremonial duties during the withdrawal period.

WITH HERD:
  - Vet writes it in a book
  - Hopes the yard staff check before putting the horse on the board

WITH PADDOCK:
  - Vet records the medication: name, dosage, route, batch number,
    withdrawal period in days
  - PADDOCK automatically calculates the withdrawal end date
  - The horse appears on the Dashboard's "Active Withdrawals" panel
    until the withdrawal period expires
  - Anyone checking the horse's profile can see the withdrawal status
    under the Medication tab

  No horse goes on parade during a withdrawal period unless someone
  actively overrides the visible warning.


================================================================================
USE CASE 7: AUDIT TRAIL — "WHO CHANGED THAT?"
================================================================================

SCENARIO: A horse's task readiness was changed from FULL EXERCISE to
LIMITED ROLE. The CO wants to know who changed it and why.

WITH HERD:
  - No audit trail
  - Ask around until someone remembers

WITH PADDOCK:
  - Every change to every record is logged automatically
  - The audit log records: who made the change, when, what the value
    was before, and what it was changed to
  - Admins can view the full audit log
  - This is not optional or configurable — it happens on every write
    operation

  Full accountability. No ambiguity.


================================================================================
USE CASE 8: DOCUMENT MANAGEMENT
================================================================================

SCENARIO: An officer needs to see a horse's passport or a vet certificate.

WITH HERD:
  - Filing cabinet, or a shared drive folder that nobody maintains

WITH PADDOCK:
  - Each horse's profile has a Documents tab
  - Documents are categorised: Passport, Injury Photo, Vet Certificate,
    Document, Other
  - Uploaded with attribution (who uploaded, when)
  - Accessible from anywhere — no need to be in the office

BOTTOM LINE
-----------
HERD tells you what horses you have. PADDOCK tells you what you can do
with them, what's wrong with them, what's about to go wrong with them,
and who is responsible. It was designed by someone who does this job,
for people who do this job.
